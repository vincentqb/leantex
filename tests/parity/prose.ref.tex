% The reference half of prose.tex. Everything the engine's source declares
% is declared here in LaTeX's own vocabulary, and declared in big points:
% LaTeX's "pt" is 1/72.27 in and the engine's is 1/72 in, so a reference
% written at 10pt sets 0.375 % smaller than the document asked for. Pinning
% the units here removes that from the comparison instead of tolerating it.
%
% The paragraph gap with no indent is the engine's paragraph model, not a
% preference: it separates paragraphs by a gap and never by an indent, so an
% indent here would be a difference in the pairing rather than between the
% engines.
\documentclass{article}
\usepackage[letterpaper,margin=72bp]{geometry}
\usepackage{fontspec}
\setmainfont{OpenSans-Regular.ttf}[Path=../corpus/fonts/]
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{6bp}
\pagestyle{plain}
\AtBeginDocument{\fontsize{10bp}{12bp}\selectfont}
\begin{document}
The harbour office keeps two ledgers, and the clerk who reconciles them
each morning has never once found them to agree before noon. One counts
hulls; the other counts berths. A hull may wait outside the wall for a
week, and a berth may stand empty while its tenant is repaired, so the two
numbers drift apart by the ordinary working of the port rather than by
anyone's error.

Write to the harbourmaster at berths@example.org if a discrepancy matters
to you. The reply will quote both ledgers and decline to prefer either,
which is the correct answer and rarely the useful one.
\end{document}
